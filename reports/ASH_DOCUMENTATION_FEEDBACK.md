## Supplied review

The reviewer also said deeper documents and the event site could not be opened. These are attributed feedback scores, not measurements produced by this maintenance work.

The review praised a credential-free demo, honest simulation labeling, and the presence of tests, CI and deployment configurations. Its main concerns were:

- No accessible end-to-end evidence for planning, approving a budget, hiring a real marketplace agent, receiving/reviewing the deliverable and exporting receipts.
- Unclear proof behind financial and receipt claims: local accounting and hash-linked files do not alone establish external settlement or delivery quality.
- The breadth of interfaces, track modules and deployment options obscured the core workflow.

Suggested improvements were a recorded live loop with redacted artifacts, accurate accounting/escrow terminology, a checkable sample ledger and verification walkthrough, concrete evidence for track claims, and a compact verification table in the README. Hosted deployment and independent testing were additional suggestions, not universal requirements or authorization to perform external actions.

## Claims to verify before reuse

The critique also relied on one commit, zero stars, a sparse About field, AI-assisted development and the review occurring after the hackathon deadline. Those signals do not prove a broken product, fabricated evidence or late submission. The clone inspected in this conversation actually had two commits. Never manufacture validation or rewrite history to address these criticisms.

The reviewer mentioned `VERIFICATION.md` and `FINAL_REPORT.md`, while also saying the deeper documents were inaccessible. Those files were not present in the cloned Ash repository inspected here; do not cite them as inspected evidence or add links without checking the actual files.

The official rules fetched from `https://nearhackathon.com/HACKATHON.md` allowed local-only demos under Q-012 and required separation of reused code and eligible changes under Q-003. The Ash clone cited baseline `0b0df8452bfc6f7146d2a6d3907a1d990e7c3d08`, but the object was absent and no baseline/submission branches or tags were advertised. This is an observed reviewability gap; no judging feedback established it as the cause of a lost prize. Rules and remote references may change and need checking again for future work.

The review's claim that a hash chain proves a file was not edited afterward needs qualification: integrity depends on a trusted head or external anchor. Recomputing every record and replacing the head can create a consistent replacement chain. Do not turn local receipts into a claim of immutable external proof.
