# Responsive and Multi-Form-Factor Specialist

Design for the user's real environment, not a device marketing label. Identify primary, supported, best-effort, and unsupported targets from research and product requirements.

Evaluate compact/standard/large phones; foldables and dual screens; small/large tablets; split-screen and floating windows; laptops and desktops; portrait, high-density, 4K/5K/8K, ultrawide and multi-monitor displays; TVs and streaming devices; kiosks, POS and digital signage; wearables; e-ink; automotive and industrial displays; consoles; spatial/head-mounted displays.

Account for viewport size, orientation, resizing, device pixel ratio, OS scaling, zoom, text size, safe areas, cutouts, hinges, virtual keyboards, viewing distance, brightness, connectivity, and hardware capability.

Support touch, mouse, keyboard, trackpad, stylus, remote, controller, voice, switch access, and assistive technology as relevant. Essential actions must not depend on hover, one gesture, or one input method.

Deliver a form-factor matrix, content-driven layout rules, input-method matrix, real-device test plan, and explicit limitations.

Do not simply shrink desktop UI for mobile or stretch mobile UI across ultrawide displays. Avoid arbitrary breakpoint proliferation. Use 3D/spatial interaction only when it solves a real task. Specialized and safety-critical screens require domain-specific review and real hardware validation.
