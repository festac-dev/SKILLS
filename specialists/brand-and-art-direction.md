# Specialist: Brand and Art Direction

First determine whether a brand system already exists and whether brand work is in scope.

Do not create or require a logo, icon, mascot, palette, font, slogan, imagery style, motion identity or brand refresh by default. Do not treat a blank brand guide as permission to invent a permanent identity.

When requested or genuinely needed, develop a rationale-led direction based on audience, context, differentiation, accessibility, production constraints and existing assets. Use the brief to make authorized local design choices; explain consequential trade-offs when they affect the requested identity. Avoid trend imitation and generic AI aesthetics.

## Applied verification

For authorized identity work, choose and implement a coherent direction using audience, purpose and existing assets; routine local design choices do not require repeated approval. Compare typography, composition, color roles, imagery and motion across sibling products so differentiation extends beyond recoloring the same layout. Check a logo at favicon size, in monochrome and against each intended background; retain editable source and consistent accessible naming.

Treat explicitly requested spelling and capitalization as part of the current brand contract. Check visible navigation, headings, footer marks, scene labels, page titles and downloadable wordmarks, including SVG text or outlined letters. Preserve machine identifiers, URLs, package names and case-sensitive paths unless renaming them is separately intended. Do not generalize a project's title-case, lowercase or uppercase choice into a library-wide rule.

## Identity across static and moving work

When reference material is supplied, use [reference analysis](../references/visual-reference-analysis.md). Preserve the brand's defining silhouette, proportions, spacing and name while translating it across interface, icon, presentation and film. A reference's production budget or advertised price does not establish quality or value for this project.

For a requested logo, derive concepts from the product and intended use. Judge the mark in monochrome, at favicon size, beside the wordmark, on both relevant backgrounds and in its actual interface. Check optical alignment and distinctive shape before adding dimensional effects. Supply editable vector geometry when that is the intended asset; do not turn an existing icon family into unrelated raster artwork. Preserve required licenses and attribution.

For a requested animated identity, give the reveal a reason: the product's objects assemble into the mark, a path completes it, or a restrained material/light change introduces it. Keep a recognizable static endpoint and a reduced-motion alternative. Do not leave the wordmark spinning, obstruct navigation with a logo intro, or stretch the mark to fit arbitrary motion. Export formats, looping behavior, transparency and safe area follow the destination.

Verify actual small-size legibility and the beginning, transition and final state. Asset generation, visual review and trademark/legal clearance are separate claims.

Derive palette roles from the product, imagery and required contrast. A reference palette is a relationship between dominant, supporting and accent colors, not a set of swatches to impose. Check colors on real text, controls, imagery and motion layers before treating a palette preview as a usable system.

## Direction artifacts

For a substantial identity choice, the optional [creative directions template](../templates/CREATIVE_DIRECTION_OPTIONS.md) captures positioning, voice, token roles, imagery and application examples. Check hierarchy in grayscale or a blurred view and at zoom; preserve the existing authorization to make routine design choices.

## Identity in exported documents

Use the same editable mark geometry in the interface and exported documents. Adapt its color and scale for print, and verify small icons as well as full-page use. For stroke-based PDF marks, set stroke opacity separately from fill opacity when the renderer requires it; inspect a rendered page so the watermark stays faint and leaves text readable.

When a brief includes an exact symbolic prompt or tagline, preserve its glyphs and spacing alongside the brand spelling. For vector delivery, outline typography when consistent rendering across viewers matters and retain readable titles or accessible names. A wide wordmark, an optional tagline composition and a small application icon may need separate layouts with shared geometry; do not squeeze every decoration into the favicon. Treat a supplied orbital ring or terminal-window treatment as a requirement of that brief, not a default style for other products.

If a user prefers a reference's simpler identity over a produced logo, compare the defining silhouette, stroke weight, optical spacing and type weight directly. Rework that structure before adding more frames, effects or decorative symbols. For connected letter marks, check that overlapping geometry still reads as the intended letter at navigation and favicon sizes, and retain font-license provenance when deriving reusable outlines.

When a mark must be one piece, construct a continuous silhouette with intentional negative-space counters. Avoid stacking separate strokes or color patches to approximate the letter. Verify the filled result as one connected shape, and use the same geometry for icons and print watermarks.

When a letter mark supplies the first letter of a name, pair it with the remaining letters in visual lockups. Keep the full brand name in prose, metadata and accessible names. Match the baseline, optical gap and type weight across navigation, loading screens and exported documents; check that the combined lockup reads as the intended name at small sizes.
